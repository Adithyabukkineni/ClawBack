#1 RefundShare

1 · Pitch. RefundShare reads an exporter's invoices, price-revision emails and bank receipts. It proves how much tariff cost they absorbed per US buyer, then generates the claim pack to negotiate a share of the refund the buyer just received.

2 · Type: vertical. One customer (Indian goods exporters who sold FOB/CIF to US buyers), one document set, no integrations. That is why it is finishable in 5 hours.

3 · The pain.

Sufferer: export managers and owners at Indian MSME exporters (apparel, engineering goods) whose US buyer was the importer of record.
Cost, FACT: refunds of the struck-down IEEPA tariffs go only to American importers. About $12B of the pool is tied to Indian goods, and exporters have no direct legal entitlement even though they absorbed costs through discounts (Maritime Gateway). 
maritimegateway
Cost, FACT: exporters were pushed into discounts of up to 25%. Industry advice is to seek credit notes, rebate-sharing clauses or price revisions, using invoices and tariff data to show how costs were absorbed. One exporter said buyers refused to share (The Wire). A second view is at Basis Point Insight. 
thewire
thewire
Coping, INFERENCE: Excel, email threads and goodwill. Nothing in the coverage points to a purpose-built tool.
Already exists? FACT: Skydo and Intoglo launched a service for exporters who were the importer of record (DDP) to claim directly (CXO Digital Pulse). That is a different segment from exporters who only discounted. I found no tool for the discounters (GUESS), so spend 10 minutes verifying before you say "first".




#2 Backstop

1 · Pitch. A circuit breaker between AI coding agents and your database: block, approve, snapshot, undo.

2 · Type: platform (narrow wedge). It qualifies because the wedge is one chokepoint, SQL tool calls from agents to Postgres, not "a new Replit". It is also the layer Cursor/Replit/Docker-class platforms will eventually need.

3 · The pain.

Sufferer: solo founders and small teams who give Cursor, Claude Code or Replit agents database or infra access.
Cost, FACT: in April 2026 a Cursor agent running Claude Opus 4.6 deleted PocketOS's production database volume and its backups in one Railway API call, in about nine seconds. It ignored project rules and Cursor's marketed "Destructive Guardrails" (Giskard). 
giskard
Cost, FACT: a Claude Code agent likewise deleted DataTalks.Club's production database (TechRepublic). PocketOS reportedly spent about 30 hours in crisis (Cyber Security News).

cybersecuritynews
Coping, INFERENCE: prompt rules the agent can ignore, manual backups, read-only tokens.
Already exists? FACT: Rubrik's Agent Rewind gives enterprises agent action audit and rollback (Virtualization Review). That is enterprise and after the fact. I found no self-serve gate that stops the destructive call before it runs (GUESS), so verify. 




#3 DemurrageGuard

1 · Pitch. Drop in a carrier's demurrage/detention invoice and get a compliance verdict and a ready-to-send dispute letter in about 10 seconds.

2 · Type: vertical. The rule text is public and deterministic, so an engine can judge the invoice while the LLM only reads the PDF.

3 · The pain.

Sufferer: small and mid-size US importers, forwarders and NVOCCs that receive container demurrage/detention (D&D) invoices.
Cost, FACT: since May 28, 2024 the FMC billing rule (46 CFR Part 541) requires D&D invoices to be issued within 30 days and carry mandated content. A non-compliant or late invoice means no obligation to pay as issued, and disputes have a 30-day window (Northeast Business Alliance). 
northeastbizalliance
Cost, FACT: the D.C. Circuit set aside only §541.4 (who may be invoiced) on Sept 23, 2025, and the rest of the rule stands (FMC). An FMC rule published Sept 1, 2026 exempts D&D charge claims from the three-year limit (TradLinx). 
fmc
tradlinx
Coping, INFERENCE: reading PDFs by hand, paying to avoid a fight, audit firms for big shippers.
Already exists? FACT: FreeTimeLedger lists D&D invoice audits against Part 541 (G2), and Intelligent Audit sells D&D invoice validation (case study). Your angle (GUESS, verify): per-invoice self-serve with no integrations, plus dispute and FMC-complaint packets.

2 · Type: vertical. Pure date-and-eligibility logic on a CSV, with no backend and no data leaving the browser.

3 · The pain.

Sufferer: small US importers and the small customs brokers who file for them.
Cost, FACT: as of June 29 CBP had authorized $104.29B in refunds and paid $71.06B, and Cato says many smaller firms haven't been able to get refunds through CAPE (Cato).

#5 CBAM Pack

1 · Pitch. It tells an Indian fastener or steel-wire exporter what the EU carbon border levy will cost per shipment, and generates the supplier data request that cuts it.

2 · Type: mixed (vertical plus workflow). The calculator alone is commodity. The supplier data-request loop is what could compound.

3 · The pain.

Sufferer: Indian MSME exporters of steel and aluminium articles to the EU, and the mills that supply them.
Cost, FACT: an Indian fastener exporter was told by Danish and German customers that CBAM reports were mandatory. Without verified data, EU default values apply (Implementing Regulation 2025/2621) and are often far above actual emissions. 35% of IamSMEofIndia members had already received emissions-data requests (The Wire). 
thewire
Cost, FACT: GTRI says defaults are often 30–80% above actuals, and exporters may need to cut prices 15–22% (India Sea Trade News). The government is reportedly working on a scheme to absorb 90% of MSME compliance cost, which was put at ₹15–20 lakh per MSME (Ease My Prep, summarizing Indian Express). 
indiaseatradenews
easemyprep
Coping, INFERENCE: Excel, consultants, chasing suppliers by email. GTRI notes large producers often don't share plant-level data with MSMEs. 
maritimegateway
Already exists? FACT: CBAM cost calculators exist (Greensutra), and verification consultancies like MSE GmbH (MSE commentary). Your angle (GUESS): the supplier-data loop for MSMEs.



6. Seatbelt + phantom-dependency check

Problem statement: Developers and small teams now let AI coding agents run shell commands and API calls with real credentials in reach. The agent can't tell staging from production, and nothing checks what a command will touch before it runs. A single wrong call can destroy production data. A single invented package name can bring in malicious code.

Who suffers: startup founders, freelancers and agencies running Cursor, Claude Code or Replit-style agents on live projects, in any country.
What it costs: on April 25, 2026, a Cursor agent deleted PocketOS's production database and volume backups in one API call, taking nine seconds. It was supposed to be fixing a staging credential problem. (dev.to write-up) One write-up counts nine documented cases of coding agents destroying data in fourteen months. (Adversa) On the package side, a Cloud Security Alliance note reports hallucinated package names in 21.7% of samples from open models and 5.2% from commercial ones. (CSA) An empty huggingface-cli package that models kept inventing got over 30,000 downloads in three months. (source)
How they cope today: approval prompts that people switch off from fatigue, system-prompt rules, and vendor guardrails. The PocketOS write-up says Cursor's destructive guardrails did not stop that deletion.
The gap: no cross-agent check that classifies which credentials are production before the agent uses them, blocks the destructive action, restores the data, and also vets what the agent installs.


7. LeakGate

Problem statement: Founders and freelancers ship apps built by AI app-builders on Supabase without knowing whether strangers can read or write their users' data. Fixing it once isn't enough, because a later migration can turn the protection off again without anyone noticing.

Who suffers: solo founders, agencies and small SaaS teams shipping Lovable- or Bolt-style apps, worldwide.
What it costs: one write-up reports 170 of 1,645 scanned Lovable apps (about 10.3%) exposed data, with 303 vulnerable endpoints. (Bastion) The related CVE record exists, though the supplier disputes who is responsible. (CVE record) Another write-up cites 12% of 100 vibe-coded apps exposing Supabase credentials. (Rocking Tech)
How they cope today: builder-side scanners such as Lovable's Security Advisor and Supabase's RLS advisor, manual SQL checks, or nothing. (Pluto Security)
The gap: existing scanners flag a problem but don't prove what an outsider can actually reach, and don't stop the same hole reappearing in a later deploy.



8. Squat Radar (standalone)

Problem statement: AI models repeatedly invent package names, repo names and domains that don't exist. Those names then appear in READMEs, setup docs and agent skill files. An attacker can register them first and wait for a developer or an agent to install them.

Who suffers: developers and security teams at companies using coding agents, and maintainers of agent-skill and MCP repos.
What it costs: the CSA reports 205,474 unique non-existent package names across the models tested, and a July 2026 CSA note describes a hallucinated npm package, react-codeshift, tied to AI-written skill instructions. (CSA July 2026 note) The same CSA material says no confirmed mass-exploitation campaign has been reported, so describe this as an attack surface, not an ongoing attack.
How they cope today: checking names by hand and using lockfiles and hashes. (The Decoder)
The gap: as far as I found, nothing scans the files agents read, such as READMEs and skill files, for names that are unclaimed or newly registered. I didn't open competitor product pages, so check this before you claim it.
